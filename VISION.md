# Act of Imagination: Vision Document

*A comprehensive vision for personal and social imagination through AI-assisted conversation*

---

## Philosophical Foundation

### The Name

**Act of Imagination** is a deliberate play on the Jungian practice of **Active Imagination** - a technique for dialoguing with unconscious material by giving it form and agency. We externalize internal material, give it tangible form, and watch it interact in an imagined space. This is play with serious purpose - therapeutic in structure without being therapy.

### The Tarot Inversion

Traditional Tarot uses fixed, impersonal archetypes (The Tower, The Fool) that the reader interprets personally. Act of Imagination inverts this - personal experiences crystallize into cards, but the underlying archetypes are still present. Every story can be broken into archetypes; we understand the universal pattern despite the deeply personal origin.

The user doesn't need to know Tarot vocabulary; they just need to feel the recognition when their card gets named.

### Rehabilitation, Not Emergency

This is flexing muscles and regaining ability after prolonged atrophy. Not crisis intervention - ongoing self-work, creative exploration, the soreness-that-feels-right of a muscle being used correctly.

### Symbiotic Human-AI Interaction

The AI doesn't replace human imagination - it extends the user's field of perception, helping them see deeper into their own experience the way AlphaGo opened new possibilities in the game of Go for human players. The human provides the raw material of lived experience; the AI provides pattern recognition, gentle guidance, and a responsive imaginative environment.

## The Core Experience

### Personal Introspection

Users engage in dialogue that surfaces themes, tensions, and recurring images from their inner landscape. These elements are distilled into tangible "cards" - abstract representations that can then be explored through imaginative play.

### Cards Rising from the Unconscious

The visual metaphor is water - specifically, the Magic 8-Ball. Cards form like a die rising through murky liquid:

1. **Submerged** - A shape exists in the depths. The subtle silver glint of displaced water signals something forming. You know *a card is forming* but not what it holds.

2. **Rising** - The shape clarifies as it ascends. Still obscured by water, but you can sense... is this a figure? A place? A tension?

3. **Surfaced** - The moment of breach. Swept with silver light as murky water recedes. The card resolves - name appears, essence crystallizes.

**You can't rush it.** You can shake the ball (keep talking, circle back, explore) but the reveal happens at its own pace.

### Arrangement Play

Once enough cards surface (3 or more), users enter arrangement mode:

- Place cards in slots
- Select a context lens (Crisis, Solitude, Community, Transition, Creation, Loss, Waiting, Conflict)
- AI generates an evocative narrative of how these elements would interact
- **Swap a card, see what changes**

This is the core loop. Placement. Observation. Modification. Discovery.

## The Expanded Vision: Social Imagination

### Beyond Personal Introspection

Act of Imagination began as a tool for personal exploration - surfacing unconscious material, working with inner archetypes. But the mechanisms it creates have broader application.

What if the "cards" weren't just personal archetypes, but **systemic elements**? Economic structures. Social policies. Community practices. And what if we could mix them with personal cards - the human difficulties that always complicate otherwise "straightforward" systems?

### Mixed Decks

The power emerges when we combine:

**Systemic Cards** (social/economic concepts):

- Community time banking
- Universal basic income
- Mutual aid networks
- Gift economy
- Consensus decision-making
- Hierarchical governance

**Personal Cards** (human elements):

- Egotism
- Agoraphobia
- Fear of abandonment
- Need for recognition
- Distrust of authority
- Addiction

**The Question**: What happens when we place "community time banking" alongside "egotism" in a Crisis context? How does "agoraphobia" interface with "universal basic income"? How do our human elements shift the otherwise "straightforward" economic component?

This is economic imagination. Social imagination. The ability to see how systems and humans interact before building them.

### Card Type Expansion

Beyond the original personal types (theme, figure, place, tension, object, absence), we add:

- **System** - Economic or social structures
- **Practice** - Community behaviors and norms
- **Policy** - Governance mechanisms
- **Resource** - Tangible or intangible assets
- **Difficulty** - Human challenges and limitations

## Frozen Time Simulation

> "I've always been fascinated with the premise of freezing a section of time to run it over and over, changing a few variables, and seeing how interactions and events become altered."

This is the core innovation of assembly iterations.

### How It Works

1. **Create an arrangement**: Place cards, select context, generate narrative
2. **Save the iteration**: The exact configuration is frozen - card snapshots, context snapshot
3. **Modify one variable**: Swap out a card, or change the context
4. **Generate again**: See how the narrative changes
5. **Compare side-by-side**: Understand the impact of that single variable

### Parallel Universe Exploration

Each iteration is a parallel universe. The same elements, slightly different configuration, completely different emergent behavior.

- "What if we remove egotism from this community structure?"
- "What if the context were Loss instead of Creation?"
- "What if we swap 'mutual aid' for 'market exchange'?"

Time frozen. Variables changed. Outcomes diverge.

### The Comparison View

Side-by-side narrative comparison allows users to:

- See exactly how changing one element ripples through the system
- Identify which elements are load-bearing (change them and everything shifts)
- Discover unexpected interactions between elements
- Build intuition for systemic dynamics

## Assemblies as Coherent Visions

### More Than Arrangements

Assemblies are not just arrangements - they're **complete worldviews**. A vision of how elements interact within a context. A hypothesis about reality.

When you place "community time banking," "mutual aid," and "gift economy" in a Community context and generate a narrative, you're not just arranging cards - you're articulating a vision of how a community could function.

### Shareable, Forkable, Remixable

Assemblies are building blocks for collective imagination:

- **Share**: Others can see your vision
- **Fork**: Others can take your assembly, swap elements, see what changes
- **Remix**: Combine elements from multiple assemblies
- **Evolve**: Track how a vision changes through iterations

This is how collective imagination works - not consensus, but proliferation and variation.

### Propagation

Assemblies can travel through the Zephyrium ecosystem. Others encounter them, respond to them, build on them. Your vision of "post-scarcity community" meets someone else's "resilient neighborhood" and something new emerges.

## The Zephyrium Journey

### Context Cards as First Travelers

Context cards are more universal than personal cards. While your card "The Locked Door" is deeply personal, the context "Transition" is universal. Everyone experiences transition.

User-created contexts will be the **first travelers** in the Zephyrium ecosystem:

- Created through guided AI conversation (like personal cards)
- Represent recurring life situations or particular lenses
- Can be shared, allowing others to view their cards through your context

### The Two Release Paths

Cards have two potential journeys after formation:

**Downward Release (Integration)**
The card dissolves back into the murky pool of the unconscious. It's "released" in the sense of letting go, returning to source. The system flags it as released, but it may resurface - probably will, because that's how the unconscious works. You don't delete trauma, you metabolize it.

The card returns transformed, wearing new clothes, or returns to say "we're not done yet."

This is private. Just you and the depths.

**Upward Release (Zephyrium)**
Like a paper lantern rising into the sky. The card has done its personal work and now goes to do work elsewhere. It enters the Zephyrium ecosystem with explicit consent - the user deliberately "sends it out."

The card travels, encounters other cards, participates in other people's arrangements (with mutual consent). The original creator can see where it's been - not as analytics, but as story:

*"Your card 'The Locked Door' was placed alongside 'The Patient Key' and 'The Room That Waits' in someone's transformation arrangement."*

### The Pearl Metaphor

These are pearls formed from internal irritations, as all good art is. Perfect seeds for stories and evolved works in Zephyrium. Deeply personal exploration becomes shared creative infrastructure.

### Return Journeys

Cards that travel don't just disappear. They come back with stories:

- Where they've been
- What other cards they encountered
- What arrangements they participated in
- How others responded to them

Your card is no longer just yours - it has a life, a history, a journey.

## Design Principles

1. **Calm over clever**: The UI should feel like a quiet room at night, not a dashboard.

2. **Emergence over extraction**: The AI surfaces what's already there, it doesn't impose frameworks.

3. **Tangibility**: Cards should feel real. The water should feel deep. Interactions should have weight.

4. **User agency**: The user decides when something becomes a card, which mode to enter, when to stop, how long to sit in darkness before following the lanterns home.

5. **No gamification**: No points, streaks, achievements. The reward is insight.

6. **No rushing**: No timers, no "session ending" warnings. The AI's final presence isn't a question or prompt - it's a settling. *"These are yours now. They'll be here."*

7. **Respect boundaries**: If a user deflects or refuses, that's final. Shadow material is patient.

8. **Local-first**: Works offline with localStorage. Cloud sync is optional, for those who want it.

## Technical Implementation

### Current Stack

- **SvelteKit 5** with Svelte 5 runes
- **Tailwind CSS** with moonlight-on-water palette
- **Claude API** with tool use for card detection
- **Supabase** for optional auth and persistence
- **Vercel** for deployment

### AI System Design

The conversation API uses Claude's tool use feature with three tools:

- `detect_forming_card` - When AI notices a theme/tension/figure forming
- `deepen_card` - When something previously detected gains more substance
- `propose_card_naming` - When a card is ready to be named

This allows natural card formation without rigid prompting.

### Local-First Architecture

- Sessions automatically save to localStorage
- Optional Supabase integration for cloud sync
- Migration prompt when first signing in with existing local data
- Works fully offline - Supabase just adds cross-device sync
